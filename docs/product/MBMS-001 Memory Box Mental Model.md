## 1. Purpose

MemoryBox is unlike most software.

Most software asks users to organize information.

MemoryBox helps users understand lives.

To accomplish this, users need a simple mental picture of what MemoryBox is.

This document defines that picture.

It is intentionally independent of technology, databases, and implementation.

It answers one question:

When someone thinks about MemoryBox, what should they imagine?

## 2. The Central Idea

MemoryBox is a living museum of a life.

Not a filing cabinet.

Not a photo organizer.

Not a chatbot.

Not a genealogy application.

A museum.

In a great museum...

The visitor is never overwhelmed.

The exhibits tell stories.

Objects have context.

The guide quietly helps visitors discover things they would never have noticed themselves.

MemoryBox is that guide.

### Design Principle

MemoryBox is the curator.

Never the exhibit.

## 3. The Mental Picture

Imagine walking into MemoryBox.

You are not greeted by folders.

You are not greeted by menus.

You are greeted by curiosity.

What would you like to explore today?

Everything else grows naturally from that question.

## 6. Evidence Is Invisible

Visitors are rarely looking for evidence.

They are looking for understanding.

Evidence exists behind every answer.

Photos.

Videos.

Emails.

Recipes.

Voice.

OCR.

Calendar.

GPS.

Evidence supports understanding.

It never replaces it.

## 7. Conversation Is the Front Door

MemoryBox has many capabilities.

Visitors should not have to learn them.

Conversation becomes the universal entry point.

Examples:

Tell me about Grandpa.

Show Christmas at Tom's house.

Play Mom laughing.

Find Dad's recipes.

What happened in 1986?

Conversation opens the door.

Exploration continues naturally.

## 9. Navigation Is Emergent

MemoryBox should avoid making visitors choose where to go.

Instead...

Every answer naturally exposes more of the archive.

A story reveals people.

People reveal places.

Places reveal moments.

Moments reveal artifacts.

Artifacts reveal stories.

Navigation becomes discovery.

Not menu selection.

## 11. Modes

### Guided Exploration

### Explorer Mode

Every tool.

Power users move quickly.

The archive remains the same.

### Family Mode

Simple.

### Contributor Mode

Adds knowledge.

Records stories.

Annotates.

Improves the archive.

The archive never changes.

Only the experience changes.

## 17. Mental Model Summary

When people think about MemoryBox...

They should picture:

A beautiful museum.

Filled with people.

Their stories.

The places they loved.

The moments that shaped them.

The things they treasured.

A quiet curator walks beside them.

Simply helping them discover the story that was always there.

### What We Decided

MemoryBox is a living museum, not a file system.

The four anchors are People, Stories, Moments, and Places.

Conversation is the front door.

Navigation emerges through discovery rather than hierarchy.

Timeline, Search, Review & Learn, and Expert Mode are lenses, not destinations.

Different interaction modes change the experience without changing the archive.

Every interaction participates in a continuous learning loop.

### Why We Decided It

Humans do not naturally remember their lives as folders, albums, or metadata. They remember through people, stories, places, and meaningful moments. The Mental Model aligns the product with how people actually think, creating a shared conceptual foundation for UX, engineering, AI, marketing, and future product decisions.

### Open Questions

Should Stories or People be the primary anchor in the interface, or should that depend on context?

Should MB explicitly visualize its knowledge graph for Explorer Mode users, or keep it entirely behind the scenes?

How should "Things" (artifacts, recipes, keepsakes, documents) be presented without competing with the four primary anchors?

Is there a fifth anchor—such as Life Chapters—that becomes important as the archive grows over decades?

How should multiple family members experience the same archive differently while preserving a single shared understanding?
