## Consistency callout

- Discovery loop uses **Narrative** (AI) after Question; human **Story** remains a first-class anchor/entry (MBKM / MB-RECONCILE-001).
- Layers match MBMS: Conversation → Four Anchors → Supporting → Expert tools.
- Modes change experience, never the archive.

Purpose

MemoryBox is fundamentally different from traditional software.

Traditional software organizes information into menus, folders, and pages.

MemoryBox organizes curiosity.

The purpose of this document is to define how people naturally move through MemoryBox.

This is not a UI specification.

This is not a screen design guide.

This is the architecture of discovery.

Every future interface—desktop, mobile, tablet, television, voice, augmented reality, or technologies yet to be invented—should express this architecture.

### Core Principle

People should never wonder where to go next.

MemoryBox should naturally reveal the next step.

Navigation should emerge from curiosity.

Not menus.

## 1. The Entry Points

MemoryBox intentionally has very few ways to begin.

Every experience starts from curiosity.

### Conversation

The primary entry point.

### Examples

Tell me about Grandpa.

Show Christmas at Tom's house.

Play Mom singing.

What happened during our trip to China?

What have I forgotten?

Conversation is the preferred front door.

### Continue Exploring

MemoryBox remembers where the visitor stopped.

### Examples

Continue yesterday's story.

Resume the China trip.

Return to Grandpa.

Visitors should feel like they're returning to a conversation, not reopening software.

### Person

Visitors sometimes know exactly who they want.

Grandpa.

Peggy.

Dad.

Sue.

Selecting a person begins exploration around that life.

### Story

Sometimes visitors remember experiences before people.

The China Trip.

Our Wedding.

The Family Reunion.

The House Build.

Stories become natural entry points.

### Collection

Sometimes visitors think thematically.

Recipes.

Woodworking.

Military.

Music.

Collections become another doorway into understanding.

### Review & Learn

Visitors intentionally help improve the archive.

Unknown faces.

Missing places.

Stories needing narration.

Review & Learn exists for stewardship rather than discovery.

### Design Rule

Entry points should remain intentionally small.

Everything else is discovered naturally.

### What We Decided

Conversation is the primary entry point.

Navigation is conversational rather than hierarchical.

Discovery follows a repeating loop: Question → Narrative → Evidence → Discovery → Teaching → Understanding → Next Question.

The archive is explored through four anchors: People, Stories, Moments, and Places.

Supporting concepts enrich the anchors without competing with them.

Timeline, Search, Review & Learn, Favorites, and the Knowledge Graph are lenses, not destinations.

Progressive disclosure keeps the experience approachable while preserving expert capabilities.

Different modes adapt the experience, not the archive itself.

### Why We Decided It

People do not naturally navigate their lives through folders or menus. They move from one memory to another through curiosity, association, and conversation. The Information Architecture mirrors that behavior, allowing MemoryBox to feel intuitive while still exposing powerful capabilities to experienced users.

### Open Questions

Should Conversation always be the default home experience, or should returning users resume where they left off?

Is the relationship between Stories and People contextual, or should one always take precedence?

How should the Knowledge Graph appear in Explorer Mode without overwhelming users?

What signals should trigger proactive suggestions from the curator without becoming intrusive?

Should there be additional specialized modes (e.g., Memory Care, Funeral Presentation, Genealogy Research), or should those be composed from the existing modes?
